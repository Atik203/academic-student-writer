Retrieval-augmented generators can still state claims that the retrieved passages contradict. Standard XGBoost ignores this disagreement, because its split gain uses only gradient and Hessian statistics. EC-XGB subtracts an evidence-disagreement penalty from the split gain, so splits that mix supported and unsupported claims become less attractive to the tree learner.

We evaluated EC-XGB on the two datasets described in Section 4. It raised macro-F1 by 1.9 points over the strongest baseline on Dataset-A. On Dataset-B the difference was 0.4 points, and the confidence interval included zero, so we do not claim an improvement there.

The ablation removes the penalty term and keeps everything else fixed. Macro-F1 falls on both datasets, which suggests that the penalty contributes to the gain, although the ablation cannot show how much of it is due to the evidence features themselves.
